## Landing Your First Internship on LinkedIn

This guide breaks down the conventional LinkedIn application method that worked for a BBA student with no prior corporate internship.

### Step 1: Fix Your Profile Before You Apply

*   **Headline:** Your pitch should be `[what you do] + [who you help] + [result]`, not just your title.
*   **Featured:** Add 1-2 projects or wins as proof. Hiring managers click here after your resume.
*   **About:** A 3-line summary: who you are, what you're building, and what you want.

*Actionable Advice:* Rewrite your headline to include a result, not just a role.

### Step 2: Apply to Startups, Not Big Companies

*   **Why startups:**
    1.  Founders often review apps personally.
    2.  Generalists are assets.
    3.  Decisions happen in days, not months.
    4.  Your impact is visible from week one.
*   **Filter Hack:** LinkedIn companies -> 11-200 employees + your target industry. Follow them, then apply.

### Step 3: How to Actually Apply on LinkedIn

*   **Apply Early:** Apply within 24-48 hours of posting for disproportionate attention.
*   **Write a Custom Note:** Use the cover note field. 3 lines: why them, one skill you bring, what you want to learn. Most applicants skip this.
*   **Tailor Your Resume:** Mirror the exact words in the job description. One version per role, not one for everything.

### Step 4: You Have More on Your Resume Than You Think

Reframe your experiences:

*   Ran a 100+ member community -> [[Event Marketing]] + Growth
*   Made films for college fests -> Content Strategy + Storytelling
*   Won business competitions -> Pitching + Sales Acumen
*   Built social media content -> Audience + Brand Development

*Key Takeaway:* Your resume is a positioning tool, not a history doc.

### Step 5: After You Apply, Do This One Thing

*   **What Actually Works:** After applying, find the hiring manager on LinkedIn. Connect with a short note: "Just applied for [role] — genuinely excited about what you're building."

This is one extra touchpoint on top of your application.

### You're Proposing a Collaboration

*   Target 10 companies well, not 100 randomly.
*   Be specific: reference the actual role and company.
*   Be confident: your background is more valuable than you think.
