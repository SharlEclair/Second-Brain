Notion is easy to understand if you grasp its "five dimensions of Notion templates." Many users download a template and feel overwhelmed by seeing everything at once, lacking an intuitive understanding of [[Notion]]. This guide aims to fix that.

### Dimension One: Notion as Digital Paper

We start with the simplest concept: the [[Notion page]], which is similar to [[Microsoft Word]]. The smallest unit of a [[Notion page]] is called a [[block]].

#### Types of Blocks
You can find your block options by pressing the slash (/) key. These include:
*   [[Basic blocks]]
*   [[Media]]
*   [[Database]]
*   [[Advanced blocks]]
*   [[Inline blocks]]
*   [[Embeds]]

If [[Notion]] were like [[Lego]], these would be your pieces. While there are many, the ones concerning this first dimension (the [[digital paper]] level) are primarily the [[basic blocks]], [[media]], some [[advanced blocks]], and [[inline blocks]]. These blocks are used as if you're editing on other [[digital paper]] apps like [[OneNote]], [[Microsoft Word]], [[Google Docs]], or even writing on physical paper.

In this dimension, you primarily deal with three aspects:

1.  **Text**: By typing things or using [[basic blocks]] like headers, lists, callouts, and dividers.
2.  **Aesthetics**: By using [[media]] such as images or videos.
3.  **Structure**: By using [[advanced blocks]] like columns and tabs.

This concludes the first dimension: [[Notion]] as [[digital paper]]. Stay tuned for the next dimension!
