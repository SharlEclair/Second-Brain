## [[Brining Chicken]] for Soft, Juicy Results

This guide outlines three methods to [[brine chicken]], ensuring it stays soft and juicy.

### 1. [[Dry Brine]]
- [ ] [[Chicken]]
- [ ] [[Salt]]

1. Rub [[salt]] into the [[chicken]].
2. Let it rest in the [[fridge]].

**Best for:** [[Fried chicken]] or dishes requiring a thick, loud crunch, as it pulls out extra moisture and dries the [[skin]].

### 2. [[Velvet Brine]] (Pro-level trick)
- [ ] [[Chicken]]
- [ ] [[Marinade]]
- [ ] [[Baking soda]] (tiny pinch)

1. Add a tiny pinch of [[baking soda]] to your [[marinade]].

**How it works:** Creates a chemical shield around the [[meat]], trapping juices inside and protecting [[chicken]] from drying out over high heat.
**Best for:** [[Chicken tikka]], [[kebabs]], or anything hitting a scorching hot [[grill]].

### 3. [[Wet Brine]] (Game Changer)
- [ ] [[Chicken]]
- [ ] [[Container]]
- [ ] [[Water]] (1 liter)
- [ ] [[Salt]] (8 grams)
- [ ] [[Flavor choice]] (e.g., [[lemon slices]], [[herbs]], [[spices]])

1. In a [[container]], mix 1 liter of [[water]], 8 grams of [[salt]], and your choice of [[flavorings]].
2. Submerge the [[chicken]] completely in the [[brine]].
3. Let it [[brine]] for 4 to 6 hours.

**How it works:** [[Salt]] travels deep into the [[meat]], unvining [[muscle fibers]] and force-feeding seasoning from the inside out, resulting in incredibly juicy [[chicken]].
**Result:** Even a plain [[chicken breast]] comes out juicy, making you feel like a [[professional chef]].
