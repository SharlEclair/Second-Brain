## Extracted Content
This conversation provides a detailed guide on how to handle [[salary negotiation]] during a job interview, specifically when an interviewer pushes for your current or previous salary information.

### Key Skills for Negotiation
*   [[Strategic Communication]]: Articulating your value and target compensation without revealing past salary details.
*   [[Self-Advocacy]]: Standing firm on your worth and expectations despite interviewer pressure.
*   [[Market Research]]: Understanding what your skills and experience command in the current job market.
*   [[Professionalism]]: Maintaining a polite yet firm stance throughout the negotiation process.
*   [[Decision Making]]: Taking time to review offers thoroughly before committing.

### Actionable Negotiation Strategies
1.  **Do Not Disclose Previous Salary**: When asked to share a payslip or previous salary, politely decline by stating you're happy to discuss compensation at the offer stage, but prefer to base it on the current role's requirements and your value.
2.  **Redirect to Target Range**: If pressed, express willingness to discuss your *target range* for the new role, rather than your past earnings.
3.  **Inquire About Flexibility**: When a number is offered, state your desired higher number and immediately ask about flexibility in base salary, [[bonuses]], or other components of the [[compensation package]].
4.  **Stand Firm on Your Value**: If interviewers mention other candidates accepting less, reiterate that you can only speak to the value *you* bring and your researched [[market rate]].
5.  **Always Request a Written Offer**: Never accept an offer verbally on the spot. Request the full offer in writing to review within 48 hours.
6.  **Confirm Alignment on Key Terms**: Before accepting, ensure alignment on your asking salary, [[bonus]] structure, start date, and [[leave]] policies.

### Key Negotiation Principles
*   Your value is based on what you bring to the *new* role, not your past earnings.
*   Employers who consistently push for your previous salary may be attempting to anchor your new compensation lower.
*   There is always time to review and negotiate a [[job offer]]; immediate pressure to accept is a red flag.
*   If an organization sees your value, they will be willing to meet your reasonable demands based on [[market value]].
*   A written offer protects both parties and allows for careful consideration of all terms and conditions.
